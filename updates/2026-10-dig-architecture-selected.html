October 23, 2026

DIG Architecture Selected

On October 19, the Library's Board of Trustees voted to appoint our architect. After a months-long, deliberate search, the renovation and expansion of the Main Library and the Bellevue Avenue Branch now has a design team.

At its meeting on Monday, October 19, 2026, the Montclair Public Library Board of Trustees voted to appoint DIG Architecture (also known as DIGroup Architecture) as the architect for the renovation and expansion of both library buildings under the NJEDA CAFE grant. The vote followed a unanimous recommendation from the Library's Architectural Selection Committee, and it marks the moment this project moves from planning into design.

Choosing an architect is one of the most consequential decisions in a project of this scale, so we want to share how we got here — the process, the people, and the reasons behind the choice. If you've been following along, this update picks up where “Selecting Our Architect” left off in August, when we announced that 21 firms had submitted proposals.

A quick reminder of what's at stake. This is the concurrent design and construction of two very different buildings: a gut renovation with major additions at the Main Library, Ralph Walker's 1955 mid-century modern landmark, and an addition and full renovation of the Bellevue Avenue Branch, a 1914 Carnegie library on the National Register of Historic Places. Both buildings will close at the same time to meet the four-year construction deadline tied to the CAFE grant — the largest state-supported library construction award in New Jersey history, and the first library project in the state to receive tax-credit financing. The work must achieve LEED Silver certification, navigate State Historic Preservation Office and local Historic Preservation Commission review, and keep the grant-committed program — including a teaching kitchen and a children's makerspace — intact. In short, we needed a firm that could do a great deal, at a demanding pace, without losing sight of the community it serves.

A committee built for the job

To lead the search, the Library convened an Architectural Selection Committee that was deliberately cross-functional. It brought together Library staff and Trustees, members of the Library Foundation and the Friends of the Library, Township leadership, and the Montclair Historic Preservation Commission, with professional project-management support guiding the process throughout. Several members are practicing architects, which put real design expertise directly into the room. Library Executive Director Radwa Ali facilitated the committee, whose members included the Library Board President and Vice President, the Township Mayor and a Town Councilor, the Historic Preservation Commission Chair, the Township Planner, and leaders from the Foundation and Friends. The full roster is listed in the briefing document linked at the end of this update.

The committee met nearly every week beginning in June to shape the search and see it through to a recommendation.

How the search unfolded

Rather than a two-step qualifications-then-proposals process, the committee chose a single-step Request for Proposals — RFP #26-01 — so it could weigh each firm's qualifications, design approach, and cost together, while keeping full decision-making authority. The RFP was developed and approved in June, issued publicly in the summer, and drew more than 20 submissions by August.

Every technical proposal was scored independently by committee members against a consistent rubric of eleven criteria worth 65 points in total. Those criteria rewarded the things this project most demands: New Jersey public-library experience, comparable large-scale construction, historic preservation and SHPO expertise, the ability to manage two buildings at once, the specific people assigned to the work, budget and schedule track records, community-engagement experience, sustainability, and the clarity of the proposal itself. Fees were evaluated separately, so cost never skewed the assessment of quality.

When the scores were compiled in early September, DIG Architecture ranked first, with a tight consensus among scorers — a meaningful signal that the committee saw the firm the same way. The committee then invited the five top firms to present in person.

The finalists

Five firms were shortlisted and presented to the committee in late September and early October:

  • DIG Architecture
  • Clarke Caton Hintz
  • Sage and Coombe Architects
  • Arcari + Iovino Architects
  • Paulus, Sokolowski and Sartor (PS&S)

Each firm had roughly an hour to present and a half hour for questions, speaking to their vision for both buildings, their design and community-engagement process, how they would support a first-time capital owner, and how they would handle the project's trickier demands — non-traditional spaces like the Fab Lab, café, and teaching kitchen; LEED Silver inside a historic Carnegie building; accessibility; and an aggressive schedule. Recordings were shared with the full committee so that every member could weigh in, whether or not they attended in person. It was a strong field, and the committee viewed Clarke Caton Hintz as a close second.

Why DIG

After the presentations, the committee's support coalesced around DIG. The firm led the technical scoring with an average of 52 out of 65, and it stood out in exactly the areas that matter most here: the specific personnel assigned to the project, budget and schedule performance, historic-preservation experience, and the clarity of its proposal. DIG is a certified M/DBE firm with a deep track record on New Jersey public libraries — including the Carnegie and 1960s sections of the Long Branch Public Library — and on large public works for the Union County Improvement Authority totaling more than $180 million.

Reference checks reinforced the choice. A sampling of what we heard:

Long Branch Public Library called DIG “the best professional firm that I've worked with in twenty-three years,” praising the team's responsiveness, its willingness to listen to staff, its sensitivity to historic buildings, and its accurate cost estimates — on a project that stayed within budget.

The Union County Improvement Authority described DIG as a “true partner” across a $150 million government complex and other projects, citing strong budget management and accountability, and said it would hire the firm again without hesitation.

Warren County Library described a receptive team and a beautiful result, with practical advice to keep day-to-day library function at the center of the work.

Montclair Veterans Services praised a collaborative, accessible team that handled challenges gracefully.

Taken together — the top technical ranking, the relevant experience, and the consistent, enthusiastic references — the committee reached a unanimous recommendation.

The fee

In the interest of transparency, DIG's professional-services fee is a fixed $2,050,500, submitted as a Best and Final Offer dated October 14, 2026. That figure reflects a significant reduction from the firm's earlier proposal while preserving the full scope of services, team, and consultant disciplines the committee evaluated. The committee considered the fee competitive and appropriate for a project of this scale, complexity, and dual-building nature, and the Township Planner reviewed the fee structure as part of the process.

A transparent process, start to finish

From the committee's kickoff in June to the Board's vote in October, the search moved steadily: the RFP approved and issued over the summer, more than 20 proposals received in August, independent scoring compiled in early September, a shortlist of five chosen mid-month, in-person presentations through late September and early October, a best-and-final fee and reference checks in the days that followed, and a final committee meeting on October 15 that confirmed the unanimous recommendation. DIG was then invited to introduce itself to the Board of Trustees on October 19, ahead of the vote that made the appointment official.

What happens next

With the appointment approved, Library staff and counsel will finalize the professional-services agreement consistent with the Best and Final Offer and the RFP terms. Kickoff activities will follow quickly — introductions, confirming the schedule, and launching the design and community-engagement process. A separate search will be conducted for a construction-management firm to help steer the building phase. And throughout, we'll keep designing with the community: the hundreds of resident voices that have already shaped this project will continue to guide the spaces that DIG helps us build.

This is a milestone worth celebrating — and the beginning of the real design work. We'll share more as the first drawings and engagement opportunities take shape.
